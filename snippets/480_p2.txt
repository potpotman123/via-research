Cloud-Based Software for Street Design Concepts 
FINDINGS 
1. 
AUTHORIZATION: 
D.C. Official Code § 2-354.04, 27 DCMR 1304, 1205.1, 1700 and 1701 
2. 
MINIMUM NEED: 
The Government of the District of Columbia, Department of Transportation ("District" or 
“DDOT”) has an immediate need for a cloud-based multimodal street design software for 
use by engineering and non-technical professionals, that combines bus route priority, 
cycling infrastructure, pedestrian initiatives, geospatial analysis, and stakeholder 
engagement, to design safer streets and to manage shared mobility to support the 
District’s Vision Zero initiative to end traffic fatalities by 2025. The software must create 
corridor-level street designs that integrate planning-level cross sections to aid the design 
of safer streets. 
3. 
ESTIMATED REASONABLE PRICE: 
The estimated fair and reasonable price is $109,500.00 per year. 
4. 
FACTS WHICH JUSTIFY SOLE SOURCE PROCUREMENT: 
A. In 2015, the Vision Zero Pedestrian and Bicycle Safety Fund (“Vision Zero”) (DC
Code § 50–921.20) was established in response to the US Department of
Transportation’s Mayor’s Challenge for Safer People and Safer Streets. Vision Zero
aims to create safe streets, protect vulnerable users, and prevent dangerous driving
through more effective use of data, education, enforcement, and engineering, in order
to reach zero fatalities and serious injuries to travelers of our transportation system by
2025.

B. VIA Transportation, Inc (Remix) is the only provider of a cloud-based multimodal
street design software that provides planning, design, geospatial analysis,
infrastructure, multimodal analysis, before/after studies, and equity analysis under one
software license. Additionally, this software allows non-technical staff to use
functions and to collaborate with technical staff and other stakeholders on designs
without specialized training.
C. A market survey for other software licenses was conducted and none had the ability to
create planning-level cross sections for customized segments of a plan-view roadway
and produce a corridor-level street design that integrates them.  Further, other
software licenses that exist required technical knowledge and training in engineering.
Examples of software that have some functions, but lack all the capabilities are
Streetmix and AutoCAD.  Streetmix only allows for the creation of cross-sections
which greatly limits production of fully integrated plans and AutoCAD can only be
used by trained individuals with some engineering backgrounds.
D. Remix is the only software that is cloud-based; for use by engineers and non-technical
professionals; and combines bus route priority, cycling infrastructure, pedestrian
initiatives, geospatial analysis, and stakeholder engagement, which DDOT needs to
make data-driven decisions, perform multimodal analysis, before/after studies, and
equity analyses, to move early-stage infrastructure projects forward, faster under one
system, and to design safer streets to further the District’s Vision Zero goals.
