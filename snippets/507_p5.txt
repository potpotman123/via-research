Finding the right partner
When an agency considers whom to work
with, there are a few considerations:
On-demand and dynamic
The most successful microtransit services are on-demand —
customers get rides when they want them — and fully
dynamic, meaning there are no static routes or schedules.
Powering a flexible service that is continuously optimized in
real time is a big technical challenge.
Moreover, it's also important to confirm that the technology
has been proven in similar markets in terms of population
density, origin-destination patterns, and traffic
characteristics - most microtransit algorithms are
configured to function well in only one specific kind of
market.
Scalability
It's one thing to power a dial-a-ride service with a few
vehicles; it's another thing entirely to power a microtransit
service at scale with dozens or even hundreds of vehicles
delivering hundreds or thousands of rides every day.
The ability to do the former, moreover, usually doesn't transfer
to the latter - as the number of vehicles and rides increase,
the number of possible rider/vehicle assignments and routing
options grow exponentially, which requires both an algorithm
and automated service management tools built to handle
scale.
=== page 6 ===
Built for public transit
Can the microtransit partner provide product features that
are specific to a transit agency's public mission? Will people
who go to recurring medical appointments be able to
pre-schedule rides for themselves and, if necessary, for
caretakers too? Similarly, will people in wheelchairs be able to
request wheelchair accessible vehicles?
What about low-income customers — will they be able
to access the service if they don't have smartphones or
credit cards?
Access to best practices
Technology alone doesn't a successful microtransit service
make. Since transit agencies have historically configured
service around fixed routes and schedules, even the sawiest
service planners and operations managers don't have
experience running real-time on-demand service with
dynamically-routed vehicles.
The best service plans are just academic if theyre not
effectively implemented and managed. What's the best way to
provide real-time customer service? What's the most
cost-effective way to acquire new riders?
Trying to reinvent the microtransit playbook is risky (as some
agencies have learned) - choosing a microtransit partner
with extensive experience launching, scaling, and optimizing
service could be the difference between success and failure.
Powerful data science tools
What kind of analytics and reporting services
does the partner provide?
Even a small service can generate millions of data points;
distilling actionable intelligence from data sets on that scale
requires powerful data science tools, analytical expertise, and
deep domain experience.
