Memorial Tree Planting 
Members of the Memorial Tree Planting Committee include: 
Mayor Mary Adams, Debra Hagen, John Rieder, Darnell Tyson, Lew Johnson, Nicole Amendola, 
Rosalie Drago, Frank Martinez, Ray Grimaldi, Judiann Carmack-Fayyaz, Irene Donohue, Nassau 
County Commissioner of Labor John Skinner 
 
Date of: Saturday, June 12, 2021 at 11am on the east side of the HL Dennison Building. The event 
will be live streamed on the Facebook account of the Suffolk County Legislature. 
 
Judiann Carmack-Fayyaz, landscape architect and teacher at Bridgehampton School, is leading 
Future Farmers of America in the design of the planting. She is working with Darnell and DPW on 
installation. The students are donating 5 native dogwood trees and blueberry bushes.  
This particular species of dogwood is known for having survived a disease and was almost lost 
emphasizing the symbolism of the species.  
 
We have a bronze plaque with the names of 21 workers who were lost to COVID and worked in 
transportation in Nassau and Suffolk counties, including bus and LIRR. 
 
Wellwood Memorial in Babylon is creating the plaque. Mayor Mary Adams connected us to them. 
We are working with Anthony Spadolini.  
 
The program will include county executives, Steve Bellone and Laura Curran, clergy, members of 
Hauppauge High School, acapella group will be singing the National Anthem, and Suffolk County 
Police Department is providing the bagpipes. We are welcoming families of the workers who were 
lost. Unfortunately, a number of these families were not able to have a form of memorial service 
due to COVID restrictions and were not necessarily mourned in the way that their families and 
friends would have liked. We are hoping this very special day will thank the families, wish them 
comfort and peace, and express our gratitude to them. 
 
The Suffolk County legislature passed resolution 1226 authorizing the installation of the 
memorial. And county executives, Bellone and Curran have lent their staff and their enthusiasm 
and resources to this effort as well.  
 
 
Micro Transit Pilot 
Jonathan Keyes and Alex Wallach from Suffolk County Department of Economic Development and 
Planning provided an update on the Pilot. It is a new type of transit service that looks like a small 
shuttlebus that doesn't run on a fixed route. It goes wherever it's needed. And it's going to be 
offering easy, affordable rides around Southampton beginning this summer. 
The launch date is June 15th, and the service is run through an app that lets you book rides right 
from your phone. You can get picked up in a short distance from where you are and access

destinations that were formerly served by the 10A, between Southampton village and Sag Harbor.   
It will let you connect to other locations where the S92 serves and many more locations in that 
area without needing a car.  This service is set to run on weekdays from 6:30am to 8:30pm.  
 
The price will be $2.25, and there are two different ways to pay. You can do it through a credit or 
debit card through the app where you can also purchase ride credit vouchers at the Hampton 
Jitney offices that you can do with credit or you can also do with cash. Face masks are going to be 
required as a COVID precaution. If you don't have a smartphone, you can also book through a 
phone number.  
 
You can also bring up to two additional passengers as guests. The important part is that we're 
going to be moving into the marketing phase on social media and local press as well as reaching 
out to our other partners in government to help spread the word about the service. 
 
It was asked what size and kind of vehicles are being used and how many will be in service.  
It is a partnership between Hampton Jitney and VIA, the creator of the app.   
 
Marcus Duffin asked if there are plans to expand to other towns. Mr. Keyes explained that 
discussion is part of the Reimagine Transit and the overall bus network restructuring.  
It's certainly one of a series of options in terms of the direction that the transit system goes, and 
one of the things we're excited about and having this pilot proceed the overall bus network 
restructuring is an opportunity to basically learn from it and to understand how people respond to 
it and engage with this type of service in here in Suffolk County, in an area where there have 
previously been a fixed route operating.
