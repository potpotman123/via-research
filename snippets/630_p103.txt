Oneida Nation
The Oneida Nation’s Oneida Reservation 
Comprehensive Plan (2005-2025) is 
available online. While not mandated to 
meet Wisconsin’s comprehensive plan 
or Smart Growth requirements, but the 
Nation wrote a plan to exceed those 
requirements. Several objectives and key 
points have been outlined below: 
	-
Intercommunity Cooperation: Other 
communities should compare their 
plans with the Oneida Nation’s to 
understand visions for shared or 
adjacent areas and find cooperation 
opportunities.
	-
Community Design: A goal is to 
“Define and maintain rural character 
for all Oneida Reservation lands and 
neighborhoods” through community 
design principles, noting that the rural 
district is not adjacent to Green Bay.
	-
Transportation: Goals include 
expanding road ownership within the 
Reservation and working with local 
governments, county highway depart­
ments, and the state on transportation 
issues in and around the Reservation.
	-
Governmental Coordination: A goal 
is to have all governments work 
positively and collaboratively for the 
benefit of citizens.
Land Policy
	-
Much of the Green Bay area is called 
“Neighborhood” in the Plan. Desired 
uses include all residential types, 
health and wellness, education, parks, 
playgrounds, and habitat restoration. 
Commercial and industrial uses 
(outside of small-scale) are undesir­
able. Institutional, recreation, forestry, 
and community agriculture uses are 
allowable.
	-
Along the West Mason corridor and 
the land north of the “Core District” is 
desirable for mixed-use housing. All 
other desirable uses are commercial 
and industrial. Some institutional uses 
are allowable.
	-
The Commercial Hospitality Core 
District is by the airport. While 
commercial uses are desirable, some 
residential uses and institutional uses 
are allowable. 
	-
The Duck Creek North Natural Corridor 
is along Duck Creek. The only desired 
uses are recreational and habitat 
restoration. All residential, commercial, 
institutional, and industrial uses are 
undesirable.
	-
A small portion of the Airport District 
is within Green Bay. The only desirable 
uses are industrial, trails, and habitat 
restoration.
	-
Each district description mentions that 
Green Bay’s Smart Growth 2022 plan 
could impact the district and should be 
reviewed.
Village of Allouez
The Village of Allouez is also committed 
to promoting intergovernmental cooper­
ation, working with surrounding commu­
nities, school districts, Brown County, and 
the State of Wisconsin, as seen in their 
2044 Comprehensive Plan. Several objec­
tives and key points have been outlined 
below:
	-
Coordinate municipal services 
and address mutual concerns with 
surrounding communities.
	-
Investigate ways to save money 
while maintaining efficient municipal 
services.
	-
Work with Green Bay School District 
and adjacent districts to evaluate 
future educational facility needs.
	-
Develop and enhance an off-street 
bicycle and pedestrian trail system 
with surrounding communities and 
Brown County.
	-
Work with surrounding communities, 
Brown County, and the Wisconsin 
Department of Transportation to plan 
county and state highway corridors.
	-
Identify and resolve existing conflicts 
with surrounding communities and the 
Brown County Planning Commission.
	-
Coordinate future parks and green 
space with adjoining communities and 
Brown County Comprehensive Outdoor 
Recreation Plan recommendations.
	-
The Village and Green Bay School 
District should cooperatively address 
traffic congestion, student accessibility, 
and other issues.
	-
Continue advocating for a K-8 public 
school in Allouez, if possible.
	-
Continue partnership with the Green 
Bay Metro Fire Department for cost-ef­
fective fire service.
	-
Continue sharing equipment with other 
municipalities and collaborating on 
transportation, utility, and joint coordi­
nation projects.
	-
Coordinate and collaborate with adja­
cent municipalities on shared interest 
projects like Webster Avenue, the East 
River, and park/open space areas.
	-
Establish additional Fox River Trail 
access points in cooperation with the 
Brown County Parks Department when 
opportunities arise.
	-
Continue working with Brown County 
Highway Department on county 
highway matters and the Sheriff’s 
Office on public safety.
	-
Identify additional safe pedestrian and 
bicycle crossings along Riverside Drive.
	-
If conditions change, develop a 
detailed flood study for the Fox River 
and update the East River Flood 
Study in cooperation with neighboring 
communities where new developments 
are occurring upstream.
	-
Increased overnight street parking by 
Green Bay residents on McCormick 
Street due to Green Bay’s overnight 
on-street parking prohibition.
	-
Coordinate improvements to the 
bicycle network and road connec­
tions at Monroe/Riverside. TID money 
is available in the Webster Avenue 
redevelopment area for improved bike/
ped connections; consider utilizing 
Clay in Green Bay to reach hospitals if 
Webster Avenue is not feasible.
	-
Collaborate on East River flooding and 
recreational matters.
	-
Partner on Marine Street redevelop­
ment with an intergovernmental TID, 
identified as an Allouez Development 
Opportunity Area on their future land 
use map.
	-
Open discussions between Allouez, 
Central Brown County Water Authority, 
and Green Bay Water Utility regarding 
connections between water utilities.

Village of Ashwaubenon
The Village of Ashwaubenon is currently 
updating its Comprehensive Plan. While 
an updated future land use map is not 
yet available, existing data from 2023 is 
accessible. Several objectives and key 
points have been outlined below: 
	-
Constant communication and coordi­
nation with Green Bay regarding public 
safety and emergency services during 
events at Lambeau Field and the Resch 
Complex, due to their long, continuous 
boundary.
	-
An intergovernmental agreement was 
established in 2023 for Green Bay to 
handle all maintenance and emergency 
repairs on Ashwaubenon’s traffic 
signals.
	-
The Village of Ashwaubenon is a 
wholesale customer of the Green Bay 
Water Utility for public water provision.
	-
Both the Village and City should 
continue informing each other about 
proposed redevelopment projects to 
address potential negative impacts 
early in the review process.
	-
Coordination is needed for adequate 
vehicle parking and flow, and a quality 
pedestrian environment as the stadium 
area redevelops.
	-
Maintaining open lines of communi­
cation for development activity and 
coordination opportunities is crucial.
	-
Coordination is sought for the exten­
sion of multi-use bicycle and pedes­
trian trails along Packerland Drive.
	-
Sharing equipment with neighboring 
local government units should continue 
when mutually beneficial.
	-
New development should complement 
existing and planned development 
across municipal boundaries.
	-
Opportunities for joint economic devel­
opment projects in municipal boundary 
areas should be evaluated through 
the creation of multijurisdictional tax 
increment districts.
	-
The majority of Ashwaubenon’s border 
with Green Bay is covered by TIF 
districts, and continued development, 
especially along Lombardi Avenue, is 
anticipated.
Village of Bellevue
The Village of Bellevue adopted their 
Comprehensive Plan in 2023 outlining 
land use recommendations, various 
potential development projects, and 
opportunities for intergovernmental 
cooperation. Several objectives and key 
points have been outlined below: 
	-
The plan acknowledges GBMT lines 
Route 1 and Route 10, micro-transit, 
the GBM On Demand app, and Metro’s 
paratransit service.
	-
The plan recognizes that dense 
commercial and residential develop­
ment patterns and frequently intercon­
nected streets are needed for attrac­
tive and efficient transit. Current land 
use and street patterns make providing 
additional transit service difficult.
	-
To make mass transit economically 
feasible, the village needs to establish 
recommended population densities, 
pedestrian systems, street networks, 
and land use patterns. Once these 
features are in place, the village should 
work with Green Bay Metro to design 
cost-effective routes.
	-
The Green Bay Metropolitan Area 
currently lacks passenger rail service, 
but a high-speed passenger rail line is 
planned to extend to Brown County 
via the Midwest Regional Rail Initiative 
(MRRI). Implementation of this service 
would provide another means for 
Bellevue residents to travel throughout 
the Midwest without personal vehicles. 
Localized efforts have recently voiced 
support for adding this route to the 
Amtrak National Network.
	-
The Village designates an eight-ton 
weight limit at the City line on Erie 
Road and Grandview Road.
	-
Bellevue aims to connect its trail 
system with those in Green Bay and 
other neighboring communities. The 
village coordinates with Green Bay and 
other local governments on park and 
open space matters.
	-
The Schmitt Industrial Park, a privately 
developed area adjacent to Green Bay, 
is identified as a primary economic 
activity area. Krueger International, Inc. 
(KI), a major employer, has its head­
quarters and manufacturing plant here, 
with Canadian National (CN) railroad 
access, though businesses currently do 
not use it for freight.
	-
The Village and City currently collab­
orate on road maintenance and snow 
plowing along borders and share 
equipment as needed.
	-
Other arrangements, such as estab­
lishing a joint land use planning 
committee to address development 
compatibility near their boundary, 
should be considered to avoid 
conflicts.
Village of Hobart
The Village of Hobart’s Comprehensive 
Plan shows future land use designations 
along the boundary with Green Bay as 
primarily being single-family residential 
and natural areas, with a small amount 
of agriculture. Several objectives and key 
points have been outlined below: 
	-
Explore mergers in public services to 
achieve cost-savings for both munici­
palities. 
	-
Maintain the Purchased Water Agree­
ment. 
	-
Seek mutual aid with Fire Departments.
	-
The Community Opinion Map indicates 
that “Light industrial and manufac­
turing uses are suited to areas Airport 
Drive and Florist Drive; along the 
border with Green Bay south of Mason 
Street; and, east of South Pine Tree 
Road between Fernando Drive and 
Orlando Drive.” However, this is not 
shown on the future land use map.
	-
The Village may consider participating 
in the Port of Green Bay’s plan imple­
mentation process to inform planners 
of its intentions to utilize the port and 
ensure modifications align with the 
Village’s long-term economic develop­
ment strategy. 
	-
Hobart’s proximity to Green Bay 
provides opportunities for directly 
marketing specialty agricultural prod­
ucts to local consumers.

Village of Howard
The Village of Howard’s Comprehensive 
Plan was adopted in 2022 and outlines 
future land use and objectives with Green 
Bay. Several objectives and key points 
have been outlined below:
	-
The plan notes the importance of 
fostering relationships to coordinate 
future development, especially in tran­
sitional areas.
	-
Due to Howard’s location relative to 
Green Bay and historical development 
patterns, the east side of the village 
is well-served by retail and service 
businesses. Balancing this when plan­
ning new development to the west is 
important, and public input identified a 
need for a grocery store to serve west-
side residents.
	-
Provide good public access to the Bay 
of Green Bay and Duck Creek.
	-
Redevelop Velp Avenue to be aesthet­
ically pleasing, improve operational 
safety, and encourage multi-modal 
transportation.
Town of Humboldt
The Town of Humboldt’s 2024 Compre­
hensive Plan Update is available online. 
Several objectives and key points have 
been outlined below:
	-
Work to maintain existing Town bound­
aries.
	-
Work cooperatively with other munici­
palities and governmental agencies.
	-
Work with surrounding communities 
to resolve boundary issues, coordinate, 
enhance, or obtain municipal services, 
and address other mutual concerns.
	-
Review the potential for consolidating 
municipal services with other govern­
ments.
	-
Identify and resolve existing conflicts 
with surrounding communities.
	-
Concentrate the densest future devel­
opment in identified development 
areas, including those adjacent to 
Green Bay.
	-
Efforts are mentioned multiple times to 
avoid annexation by Green Bay.
	-
Discuss cooperative planning along 
municipal borders with adjacent 
municipalities.
	-
Begin comprehensive boundary agree­
ment discussions with Green Bay.
	-
Maintain contact with the Joint City 
of Green Bay/Town of Scott Planning 
Commission regarding issues along the 
border of Green Bay and Humboldt on 
its north end.
	-
Analyze the possibility of public sani­
tary service in areas close to existing 
sewered development in Green Bay.
	-
Establish a new residential zoning 
classification adjacent to the City, with 
a minimum lot size of 1.5 acres and 200 
feet of street frontage.
	-
Communicate and coordinate trans­
portation projects and policies with 
surrounding municipalities, including 
Green Bay. Existing agreements for 
shared border road maintenance are in 
place.
	-
Work with Green Bay and Baird Creek 
Preservation Foundation to acquire 
land along Baird Creek and develop 
new multi-use trails connecting to 
Green Bay’s existing trails.
	-
Coordinate with nearby communities 
in siting businesses that are part of a 
Green Bay region business cluster or 
fulfill local employment needs.
	-
Proactively identify areas in the Towns 
at risk for annexation into Green Bay 
and work to identify alternatives to 
discourage annexation.
	-
Assess the possibility of contracting 
with Green Bay to supply potable 
water to parts of Humboldt, noting that 
the Green Bay Water Utility operates a 
water treatment plant in Humboldt.
	-
Adopt an intergovernmental agree­
ment with Green Bay addressing 
boundary and annexation issues and 
potential sanitary sewer and water 
service to the Town.
Town of Scott
While the Town of Scott is currently in the 
process of updating its Comprehensive 
Plan, the Town’s current 2017 Compre­
hensive Plan is available online. The City 
currently has a Boundary Agreement 
that fixes the Town-City borders until 
2033. This agreement has a joint planning 
area where land-use decisions are made 
together with the utilization of a Green 
Bay – Scott Joint Plan Commission. The 
Town anticipates sufficient development 
pressure within the Joint Planning Area to 
justify Green Bay providing potable water 
to the Town. The City of Green Bay and 
Town of Scott should continue coopera­
tion and communication within the Joint 
Planning Area and seek to expand it. 
Several objectives and key points have 
been outlined below:
	-
Create a town square south of Church 
Rd between Bay Settlement Rd and 
HWY 57.
	-
Develop mixed residential-commercial 
areas east of the City boundary and 
west of HWY 57, and east of the joint 
planning area north of HWY 54.
	-
Develop commercial corridors along 
HWY 57 and Algoma Rd/HWY 54, 
possibly through a corridor study.
	-
Adjust and update the joint zoning 
code to follow current best practices.
	-
Plan for quarry expansion along 
Church Rd.
	-
Develop trail systems, both on and 
off-road, with large systems along Bay 
Settlement Road and Algoma Rd/HWY 
54. Coordinate with Green Bay for 
seamless connections.
	-
Examine extending transit services to 
the urban portion of the town.
	-
Recognize farming as an important 
component of the local economy 
and encourage active farmers. Iden­
tify productive agricultural land and 
methods to encourage farming.
	-
Work with the Green Bay School 
District to identify sites for future 
schools within and near the Town.
	-
Preserve historic and scenic sites such 
as the Niagara Escarpment, Wequiock 
Creek, historic farmsteads, and the bay 
shore.
