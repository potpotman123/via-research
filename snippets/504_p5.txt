Reimagine Transit 
Darnell Tyson offered an update on the Reimagine Transit. The on-demand pilot is a component of 
the reimagine transit initiative. 
 
The project is a partnership of Economic Development and Planning and DPW working with a 
consultant Jared Walker and Associates in a process to reimagine the entire transportation system 
that we have. The study is looking at what the public would like to see in terms of a system that 
either provides coverage, meaning some service to all parts of the system, or ridership, which sort 
of focuses more frequency on a smaller number of peak routes. We had a public meeting on May 
5th, and the team will be out at a number of peak locations around the county, interacting and 
getting our customers to participate in a survey.  
 
The team has created an online survey to gather public input on service that's currently provided. 
Right now the system is half coverage, half ridership. The survey is asking whether or not you 
would like to keep it that way or if you would like to shift more towards the coverage portion or 
the ridership network. The survey will be open until I believe the end of May.  
 
Mr. Keyes added, regarding the survey, that some of the comments heard earlier today, regarding 
the ability to contrast the survey map, and for those who may be visually impaired in the ability to 
interpret the map. The map is now revised so that the potential high frequency routes are now in a 
color scheme, that there's greater contrast between them, and try to really enhance the contrast 
overall on the map to address that issue, and those new maps are now currently on the 
reimagined transit website, and are also reflected in the updated choices report. And the other 
thing was with the overall navigability of the website itself, we're adding additional links to make 
it easier to find the choices report and the survey. And we've got a few more coming so that when 
you hit the landing page of Connect Li, you can find the information a little more easily. 
 
Legislator Fleming asked that since this is a public meeting, and for folks who might not have been 
involved early on, what is the difference between the ridership model and the coverage model? 
 
Darnell explained using an example of 18 buses.  If you have a coverage system, you have 18 
buses, and you have nine routes, you would be putting two buses on each of the routes. So you get 
a bus everywhere around the system, but it is not a frequent service. The ridership system is with 
those same 18 buses, but you would not have nine routes. There would be two routes, but you 
would have nine buses on each route.
