1.0 
INTRODUCTION AND PROGRAM BACKGROUND 
1.1 
Commitment to Excellence 
The City of Lompoc takes great pride in the mobility services it provides to persons living in, 
working in, or visiting the Lompoc Valley. Many residents, including youth, older adults, and 
persons with disabilities, have come to rely on these services as a means of transportation to 
school, work or medical appointments. The City remains committed to providing mobility services 
that are effective in meeting the needs of the community, provide value to the overall community, 
and make prudent use the City's transit funds. 
 
1.2 
Historic Overview 
The City of Lompoc has been providing public transit service to the Lompoc Valley since 1976 via 
a general public demand-response service, which later evolved into a fixed-route service and an 
eligibility-based demand-response service (Dial-A-Ride).  
 
Across a 20-year period (2000-2020), the City’s public transit program was subject to a variety of 
service changes.  The more significant changes included periodic route modifications, introduction 
of the Wine County Express linking the Lompoc Valley with the Santa Ynez Valley, introduction 
of the Santa Barbara Shuttle providing access to healthcare centers and cities in the south county, 
expansion of scheduled bus service linking the Lompoc Valley with Santa Maria, and transition of 
the Clean Air Express program to the Santa Barbara County Association of Governments.  
Ridership remained strong during this period. 
 
The onset of the COVID-19 pandemic (Spring 2020) resulted in significant declines in both 
ridership and fare revenue.  While total ridership has rebounded to approximately 88 percent of 
pre-pandemic levels, it is clear the community’s mobility needs and priorities have changed   
Further, the City is now subject to the state mandate requiring transition to zero-emission vehicles. 
Taken collectively, these factors served as catalyst for a top-to-bottom examination of the City’s 
public transit program which was completed during the second half of 2024. 
 
In November 2024, the Lompoc city council directed staff to implement a new transit program 
inclusive of the following: 
 
• 
Redesign COLT local fixed routes (Routes 1, 2, and 3) to have a more east-west focus.  
 
• 
Introduce a H St. Shuttle (Route 5) to provide frequent north-south service along the City’s 
primary arterial between the Lompoc Transit Hub and Mission Plaza. 
 
• 
Retime the Route 4 (Vandenberg Village) schedule to better integrate with the other fixed 
routes.  
 
• 
Introduce general public on-demand service on weekday evenings (replacing fixed-route, 
ADA Dial-A-Ride, and Allan Hancock College Shuttle services during this time).  
 
• 
Introduce general public on-demand service on Saturday (replacing fixed-route and ADA 
Dial-A-Ride service during this time).

Looking Forward:  Scope of Services 
The City is seeking one prime contractor to provide day-to-day administration and operation of its 
public transit/transportation services.  Teaming is encouraged, provided one firm serves as the 
prime contractor and others as subcontractors. 
 
 
1.3 
General Public Fixed-Route Service 
The City’s fixed-route transit service includes the following elements:  three routes (Routes 1, 2, 
and 3) operating within Lompoc city limits; one route (Route 4) serving Vandenberg Village and 
Mission Hills; a limited-stop, high-frequency shuttle (Route 5/H Street Shuttle) traveling chiefly 
along H Street; an inter-community service (Wine Country Express) linking Lompoc with the 
neighboring Santa Ynez Valley; and a twice-weekly service (Santa Barbara Shuttle) linking 
Lompoc with healthcare centers in Santa Barbara and Goleta. 
 
Routes 1, 2, and 3 each operate on 40-minute service frequency using one vehicle for each 
route. These routes will operate Monday through Friday, from 6:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. and will focus 
on east-west travel in south Lompoc, central Lompoc, and north Lompoc, offering connections 
with the H Street Shuttle at multiple locations. The proposed route alignments and schedules are 
provided in Attachment 15. 
 
Route 4 will operate from approximately 6:30 am to 5:00 pm, Monday through Friday.  The 
Contractor will deploy one vehicle to operate this service. The proposed route alignment and 
schedule are provided in Attachment 15. 
 
Route 5 (the H Street Shuttle) will provide 20-minute bi-directional service from 6:00 am to 5:00 
pm Monday through Friday north and south on H Street between the Transit Hub and Mission 
Plaza. The Contractor will deploy one vehicle to operate this service.  The proposed route 
alignment and schedule are provided in Attachment 15. 
 
The Wine Country Express will continue to provide three round trips per day, Monday through 
Saturday, as currently scheduled.  One vehicle will be utilized. A link to the service brochure is 
provided in Attachment 15. 
 
The Santa Barbara Shuttle will continue to provide service between Lompoc and Santa Barbara 
on Tuesday and Thursday as currently scheduled. The shuttle currently picks up Dial-A-Ride 
passengers in Lompoc first, then picks up other riders at the Transit Hub at 8:30 a.m.  Riders are 
picked up at 1206 W. Chapala Street in Santa Barbara (next to China Pavilion) at 3:30 p.m., after 
which the shuttle picks up Dial-A-Ride passengers before returning to Lompoc. The vehicle and 
driver remain in Santa Barbara during this period. One vehicle will be assigned. 
 
The services discussed above will not operate on Saturday (except the Wine Country Express), 
Sunday or City-designated holidays.  These include  New Year’s Day, MLK Jr. Day, Presidents’ 
Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving
