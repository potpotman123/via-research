Question:
On average, how many landfill dropoffs do the City’s collection vehicles perform a day? How long do the City’s 
dropoffs typically take?
Answer:  
Residential trash 1-2 loads, Residential recycling 1 load, Residential Rearload 1 load, Residential Claw 3-4 loads, 
Commercial Frontload trash 2-3 loads, Commercial Frontload recycling 1 load and Rolloff 6-9 loads.  In general 
tipping times can take between 10-30 minutes.

Geographic Information System (GIS) integration with the City of Largo’s Esri platform is required. Open 
Representational State Transfer (REST) services integration will allow for sharing and integration of map data 
which is the preferred method of integration;
Question:
Can the City provide more information on the functionalities it seeks through integration with Esri? Are there 
specific data fields the City intends to integrate within the contemplated Waste Management platform?
Answer:
We are looking to integrate with ESRI so that the Solid Waste vehicles will be on a map layer that our PW Fleet 
Division can access for work orders within our Asset Management software. Also, if your software allows for ESRI
integration, we can publish map layers such as road closures to the route planning software. If your software 
solution allows for integration, we would need a webservice published out that we can access.

Page 16, RFP Text:
Describe hardware required, including whether equipment is hardwired into the trucks and any mounts, cases, or 
other systems required
Question:
Can the City provide information regarding any data collection hardware currently installed on its  vehicles? Do 
the City’s vehicles have hardware such as scales to weigh collection, cameras, or any other data collection 
mechanisms?  Will there be power sources available in the City’s collection vehicles? 
Answer:  
All Residential trash and recycling trucks have RFID readers.  All trucks have Samsung E8 tablets with mounts in 
the trucks.  There are no scales, cameras (other than the tablets) or other devices other than stated.  Power is 
currently supplied by the truck battery for charging purposes.

Question:
What is the City’s current process for accepting and routing call-in pickup requests? What is the typical volume of
call-in requests per day? Are these requests typically geographically dispersed or concentrated?
Answer:  
The City accepts Roll off work via phone or e-mail, work orders are created and given to the Supervisor for 
manual geographic routing.  Call in requests range from 14-18 per day.
