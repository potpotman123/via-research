River North Transit, LLC  aka Via On Demand Utah Transit Service (Microtransit) 
Microtransit is a flexible, on-demand transportation service that uses smaller vehicles to connect people 
to larger public transportation systems. UTA has an ongoing service contract with Via to operate the On 
Demand Microtransit service. Via provides this service in five zones: Southern Salt Lake County, Salt 
Lake City West Side, South Davis County, West Provo, and Tooele County. In Tooele County, Via 
solely provides mobile phone technology for operations of UTA On Demand (operated by the county).  
The Salt Lake City West Side Zone is 100% funded by Salt Lake City.   In the remaining zones, Via 
provides mobile phone technologies, drivers, vehicles, maintenance, operations support and customer 
service solutions. 
Rocky Mountain Power 
Rocky Mountain Power has provided funding for UTA research and infrastructure development 
projects. These fall within five main areas of focus: energy efficiency, electric vehicles, electrical 
infrastructure, grid resilience, and research and grants. In collaboration with RMP, UTA has 
successfully advanced the development of a regional bus charging network. RMP, Utah State 
University, and UTA have partnered on several grants to fund initiatives like the development of UTA’s 
battery bus chargers and research electrification. RMP also completed the development of the 
WattSmart Plan to help reduce energy usage at UTA. Other accomplishments include air quality 
monitoring projects, and many grant applications.  In March 2023, Utah's SB125 established the 
framework for these collaborative efforts. UTA is a member of the SB125 Steering Committee. 
HNTB 
HNTB provides program management services to UTA. These services primarily include supporting the 
Capital Services and Planning Departments. Support services include assisting the UTA project 
managers with day-to-day management of their projects, providing technical/design oversight, 
construction management and oversight, procurement support, and project controls functions. In 
addition to project support, HNTB provides specialized services including updates to UTA standards 
and specifications, updating vehicle management plans, assisting with UTA’s 5-year, 10-year, and long-
range planning documents, and support with IT related programs/tools focused on project management. 
Within the Innovative Mobility Team, HNTB contractors act as a staff extension by attending IMS team 
meetings and maintaining regular hours at UTA.  
Contractors provide support on miscellaneous projects including research, memo writing, project charter 
development, stakeholder coordination, and other tasks.

Special Services 
Utah Transit Authority has contracted with multiple organizations in public/private partnerships to 
provide Flex Route and/or Paratransit service.  These public/private partnerships facilitate transit service 
for our customers with disabilities and the public throughout the region. In Weber, Davis and Box Elder 
Counties, UTA contracts with MV Transportation Inc for Flex Route and Paratransit service. In the Utah 
County area, UTA contracts with United Way for Paratransit service. In Tooele County, UTA contracts 
with Via Transportation for the technology component and Tooele County for operations of On Demand 
service and a mid-day Tooele to Salt Lake Flex Route. 
 
Van Pool Services 
 
UTA also participates in public/private partnerships throughout the region with multiple vendors 
providing all maintenance and tire services for the entire fleet of over five hundred van pool vehicles. 
Additionally, UTA has 34 public/private vanpool agreements that facilitate groups of employees 
commuting to work. These include employers throughout the region and most of the ski resorts 
providing canyon transportation for their employees. 
 
Transportation Network Company (TNC) 
 
UTA has contracts with Lyft, Uber, and Yellow Cab to provide rides as needed.  UTA uses services 
from these providers for a variety of purposes including ReConnect, a program UTA developed to 
provide rides for customers in the event of an in-system breakdown or equipment failure.  UTA also 
uses these vendors to provide overflow trips for UTA On Demand transit services.  UTA uses TNC 
providers for Paratransit service overflow trips as well.    
 
Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) 
 
UTA partners with publicly selected, private development companies to implement high-intensity, 
mixed-use development around transit stations. These relationships are generally in the form of joint 
ventures or ground leases, the revenue of which may be used for transit-related purposes as identified by 
UTA’s Local Advisory Council and the Board of Trustees. UTA has active TOD projects in Ogden (J 
Fisher Co – Union Station Neighborhood), Clearfield (STACK Real Estate and Hamilton Partners – 
Clearfield Station TOD), South Salt Lake (Columbus Group – The Hub of Opportunity), Sandy 
(Hamilton Partners – The East Village), and South Jordan (Thackeray Garn and STACK Real Estate – 
SoJo Station). Several more partnerships are anticipated for the large Station Area Planning effort 
initiated by 2022 HB462. Such planning and development are designed to meet the objectives of 
increasing the availability and affordability of housing, promoting sustainable environmental conditions, 
enhancing access to opportunities, and increasing transportation choices and connections.
