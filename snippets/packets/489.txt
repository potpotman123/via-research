1.0 
INTRODUCTION AND PROGRAM BACKGROUND 
1.1 
Commitment to Excellence 
The City of Lompoc takes great pride in the mobility services it provides to persons living in, 
working in, or visiting the Lompoc Valley. Many residents, including youth, older adults, and 
persons with disabilities, have come to rely on these services as a means of transportation to 
school, work or medical appointments. The City remains committed to providing mobility services 
that are effective in meeting the needs of the community, provide value to the overall community, 
and make prudent use the City's transit funds. 
 
1.2 
Historic Overview 
The City of Lompoc has been providing public transit service to the Lompoc Valley since 1976 via 
a general public demand-response service, which later evolved into a fixed-route service and an 
eligibility-based demand-response service (Dial-A-Ride).  
 
Across a 20-year period (2000-2020), the City’s public transit program was subject to a variety of 
service changes.  The more significant changes included periodic route modifications, introduction 
of the Wine County Express linking the Lompoc Valley with the Santa Ynez Valley, introduction 
of the Santa Barbara Shuttle providing access to healthcare centers and cities in the south county, 
expansion of scheduled bus service linking the Lompoc Valley with Santa Maria, and transition of 
the Clean Air Express program to the Santa Barbara County Association of Governments.  
Ridership remained strong during this period. 
 
The onset of the COVID-19 pandemic (Spring 2020) resulted in significant declines in both 
ridership and fare revenue.  While total ridership has rebounded to approximately 88 percent of 
pre-pandemic levels, it is clear the community’s mobility needs and priorities have changed   
Further, the City is now subject to the state mandate requiring transition to zero-emission vehicles. 
Taken collectively, these factors served as catalyst for a top-to-bottom examination of the City’s 
public transit program which was completed during the second half of 2024. 
 
In November 2024, the Lompoc city council directed staff to implement a new transit program 
inclusive of the following: 
 
• 
Redesign COLT local fixed routes (Routes 1, 2, and 3) to have a more east-west focus.  
 
• 
Introduce a H St. Shuttle (Route 5) to provide frequent north-south service along the City’s 
primary arterial between the Lompoc Transit Hub and Mission Plaza. 
 
• 
Retime the Route 4 (Vandenberg Village) schedule to better integrate with the other fixed 
routes.  
 
• 
Introduce general public on-demand service on weekday evenings (replacing fixed-route, 
ADA Dial-A-Ride, and Allan Hancock College Shuttle services during this time).  
 
• 
Introduce general public on-demand service on Saturday (replacing fixed-route and ADA 
Dial-A-Ride service during this time).

1.4 
ADA Complementary Paratransit Service 
The City’s ADA complementary paratransit service (Dial-A-Ride) is a curb-to-curb, shared-ride 
service provided for residents of the Lompoc Valley possessing an ADA recognized disability.  
Physician certification is required in order to be eligible to use the service. 
 
The service will operate Monday through Friday, 6:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.  Service will initially be 
provided with one vehicle, with the potential for more to be added should demand warrant. The 
service span mirrors that of the fixed-route service. 
 
The service will not operate on Saturday, Sunday, or City-designated holidays:  New Year’s Day, 
MLK Jr. Day, Presidents’ Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, 
Veterans Day, Thanksgiving Day, Day after Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, and Christmas Day. 
 
The City will continue to be responsible for all eligibility and registration for the ADA Paratransit 
service. Registration applications and waivers must be submitted to the City for review and 
approval.  Approval is required before the rider can use this service. Applications are typically 
processed within three business days of receipt, and notification of approval is provided by mail.  
 
A personal care attendant (PCA) may accompany a fare-paying  rider for no additional fare.  By 
contrast, a person traveling as a companion must pay the same fare as the rider they are 
accompanying. 
 
ADA Paratransit reservations shall be supported by the Contractor’s website and mobile customer 
service portal, as detailed in the Scope of Work. User-friendly features shall enable customers to 
quickly and easily schedule, purchase, and manager their trips and accounts. These tools will 
support both on-demand and scheduled trip booking.  Riders must also be able to book a trip via 
telephone. Bidders are to discuss their proposed ride scheduling terms and policies within their 
respective proposals. 
 
A late cancellation is defined as one that is received less than two hours before the requested 
pick-up time. A no-show is defined as a trip that is not cancelled or one that is cancelled after the 
vehicle has arrived at the pick-up location. Drivers will wait three (3) minutes after the scheduled 
pick-up time.  Excessive late cancellations or no-shows may result in suspension or loss of riding 
privileges. 
 
1.5 
General Public On-Demand Service 
The Contractor will operate a general public on-demand service within Lompoc city limits as well 
as in Mission Hills and Vandenberg Village.  This service will utilize a “corner-to-corner” service 
approach focusing on safe rider pick-up and drop-off at nearby intersections in close proximity to 
the origin and destination points. The locations of “corner-to-corner” virtual stops will be 
determined by the successful bidder’s dispatching algorithm, which will identify safe locations 
(such as nearby intersections or side streets) for the vehicle to stop. ADA-certified riders will be 
eligible to receive curb-to-curb service.

1.21 
Telephone and Technology 
1.21.1 Telephone Numbers 
The CITY will provide the CONTRACTOR with the Customer Service and reservation numbers 
for the ADA paratransit service as well as the general public on-demand service.  
 
CONTRACTOR shall provide customer information service to the public during all hours of service 
operation. CONTRACTOR shall also have the ability to accept calls from hearing-impaired 
persons as well as persons with limited English proficiency. 
 
All phone lines associated with this contract shall be used solely for the purpose of providing 
customer information and accepting ride reservations, and shall not be used by the 
CONTRACTOR for any other purpose. 
 
Upon termination of this agreement the CONTRACTOR shall release such phone numbers to the 
CITY.  
 
1.21.2 Software Backend Characteristics 
Software must provide for scheduling, dispatch, reservation, user interface, and data collection. 
Bidders proposing to leverage existing technology and utilize proprietary software must indicate 
how it meets these requirements. The system must offer a call-in reservation option as well as a 
self-booking option through a mobile app and website. Advance reservations must be available 
for the ADA paratransit service and general public on-demand service. Riders will have the option 
to pay online, via a mobile app, or onboard the vehicle. 
 
The technology must have the ability to limit trip requests based on geography (within an 
established service area) and time (within established service hours). It must also be able to 
schedule curb-to-curb service (ADA paratransit) and designate safe and appropriate “virtual 
stops” for corner-to-corner service (general public on-demand service). 
 
CONTRACTOR shall be responsible for creating the GTFS “feed” and providing and integrating 
any software and hardware necessary to provide the real-time service information and functions 
required below, to work seamlessly with the customer webpage and mobile apps, as well as with 
Google Transit.  The real-time software shall integrate with the CITY’s existing vehicle telematics 
hardware and software already in use, and/or any upgrades to CITY hardware and software as 
needed.  The CONTRACTOR shall deploy the real-time software, support the real-time software, 
and update the real-time software.  CONTRACTOR shall include in its proposal an explanation of 
its cybersecurity plan and proposed framework of system and operation.

application on behalf of the CITY’s public transit program. The scope and content of these 
technologies will include all transit programs operated by the CITY.

• 
Ability to identify a Personal Care Attendant (PCA).
