Public 
Transportation
Providing residents with an affordable, 
accessible, and eco-friendly alternative to 
personal vehicles is a key component to 
meeting alternative transportation goals 
and serving those in the community who 
may not want or have adequate, reliable 
private transportation. Governed by the 
Green Bay Transit Commission, Green 
Bay Metro (GBM) provides services to the 
City and other neighboring communities 
such as De Pere, Allouez, Ashwaubenon, 
and Bellevue. GBM currently operates 11 
full-service bus routes converging at the 
Green Bay Transit Center on University 
Avenue, a hub for local and intercity 
transit.
However, as noted in community 
outreach, service gaps within the transit 
network are still perceived within the 
community and will require effective 
education and outreach to minimize 
these perceptions. It is important to 
acknowledge Green Bay’s lower popula­
tion density, which presents challenges 
for operating and expanding a high 
frequency and extensive transit system. 
Lower density communities can make it 
difficult to achieve ridership levels often 
needed to justify more frequent routes 
and broader coverage. 
GBM On Demand
Green Bay Metro and Via Transportation 
have launched GBM On Demand, Wiscon­
sin’s first micro transit service. Beyond 
GBM’s fixed routes, GBM On Demand 
provides microtransit services offering 
affordable, efficient, and convenient 
shared rides near residential neighbor­
hoods, key transit hubs, and designated 
commercial and medical centers. 
GBM On Demand allows for more 
flexibility in providing transit services 
than fixed routes and better addresses 
changing demands in the community. 
This shared-ride system complements 
existing transit by offering affordable, 
convenient travel near neighborhoods, 
transit hubs, and key destinations. Riders 
can book trips in real time via the mobile 
app or phone, with wheelchair-accessible 
options available. 
Micromobility 
Green Bay partners with Bird Rides to 
offer shared e-scooters and e-bikes, 
expanding transportation options and 
enhancing mobility across the City. 
These services provide a convenient, 
eco-friendly alternative for short trips 
while reducing traffic congestion and 
promoting active transportation. Along 
with these services, every vehicle in the 
GBM fleet can accommodate bicycles. 
Integrating micromobility across mobility 
types and existing infrastructure supports 
a more connected and accessible 
community.
Recommendations 
1.	 Continue to monitor current service 
and explore potential expansion 
opportunities for the community.
2.	Continue the LIFT Program, offering 
riders that meet the income require­
ment up to four day passes per 
month. 
3.	Continue to upgrade bus stops with 
shelters, seating, and lighting to 
improve the transit experience where 
appropriate.
4.	Provide public transportation routes 
to connect residents and visitors to 
new destinations throughout the City, 
where feasible. 
5.	Work with Green Bay Metro 
to promote public awareness 
campaigns to highlight the benefits 
of public transportation and increase 
transit ridership.
6.	Partner with micromobility providers 
to expand the availability and variety 
of shared transportation options, 
ensuring coverage across more 
neighborhoods facilitating more 
seamless first-mile/last-mile connec­
tions.
7.	 Promote transit-oriented develop­
ment around the transit hub facility 
Downtown or another feasibly 
facility. 
8.	Work with GBM’s mobility manager 
to promote education and outreach 
of the transit system, focusing on 
regular routes, availability of on-de­
mand microtransit, and smart tech­
nology integration.
9.	Develop a recruitment plan to 
expand the number of GBM bus 
drivers, focusing on training individ­
uals and developing talent. 
10.	Maintain the Transit Asset Manage­
ment Plan along with the Capital 
Improvement Plan and fleet replace­
ment schedule.

Village of Ashwaubenon
The Village of Ashwaubenon is currently 
updating its Comprehensive Plan. While 
an updated future land use map is not 
yet available, existing data from 2023 is 
accessible. Several objectives and key 
points have been outlined below: 
	-
Constant communication and coordi­
nation with Green Bay regarding public 
safety and emergency services during 
events at Lambeau Field and the Resch 
Complex, due to their long, continuous 
boundary.
	-
An intergovernmental agreement was 
established in 2023 for Green Bay to 
handle all maintenance and emergency 
repairs on Ashwaubenon’s traffic 
signals.
	-
The Village of Ashwaubenon is a 
wholesale customer of the Green Bay 
Water Utility for public water provision.
	-
Both the Village and City should 
continue informing each other about 
proposed redevelopment projects to 
address potential negative impacts 
early in the review process.
	-
Coordination is needed for adequate 
vehicle parking and flow, and a quality 
pedestrian environment as the stadium 
area redevelops.
	-
Maintaining open lines of communi­
cation for development activity and 
coordination opportunities is crucial.
	-
Coordination is sought for the exten­
sion of multi-use bicycle and pedes­
trian trails along Packerland Drive.
	-
Sharing equipment with neighboring 
local government units should continue 
when mutually beneficial.
	-
New development should complement 
existing and planned development 
across municipal boundaries.
	-
Opportunities for joint economic devel­
opment projects in municipal boundary 
areas should be evaluated through 
the creation of multijurisdictional tax 
increment districts.
	-
The majority of Ashwaubenon’s border 
with Green Bay is covered by TIF 
districts, and continued development, 
especially along Lombardi Avenue, is 
anticipated.
Village of Bellevue
The Village of Bellevue adopted their 
Comprehensive Plan in 2023 outlining 
land use recommendations, various 
potential development projects, and 
opportunities for intergovernmental 
cooperation. Several objectives and key 
points have been outlined below: 
	-
The plan acknowledges GBMT lines 
Route 1 and Route 10, micro-transit, 
the GBM On Demand app, and Metro’s 
paratransit service.
	-
The plan recognizes that dense 
commercial and residential develop­
ment patterns and frequently intercon­
nected streets are needed for attrac­
tive and efficient transit. Current land 
use and street patterns make providing 
additional transit service difficult.
	-
To make mass transit economically 
feasible, the village needs to establish 
recommended population densities, 
pedestrian systems, street networks, 
and land use patterns. Once these 
features are in place, the village should 
work with Green Bay Metro to design 
cost-effective routes.
	-
The Green Bay Metropolitan Area 
currently lacks passenger rail service, 
but a high-speed passenger rail line is 
planned to extend to Brown County 
via the Midwest Regional Rail Initiative 
(MRRI). Implementation of this service 
would provide another means for 
Bellevue residents to travel throughout 
the Midwest without personal vehicles. 
Localized efforts have recently voiced 
support for adding this route to the 
Amtrak National Network.
	-
The Village designates an eight-ton 
weight limit at the City line on Erie 
Road and Grandview Road.
	-
Bellevue aims to connect its trail 
system with those in Green Bay and 
other neighboring communities. The 
village coordinates with Green Bay and 
other local governments on park and 
open space matters.
	-
The Schmitt Industrial Park, a privately 
developed area adjacent to Green Bay, 
is identified as a primary economic 
activity area. Krueger International, Inc. 
(KI), a major employer, has its head­
quarters and manufacturing plant here, 
with Canadian National (CN) railroad 
access, though businesses currently do 
not use it for freight.
	-
The Village and City currently collab­
orate on road maintenance and snow 
plowing along borders and share 
equipment as needed.
	-
Other arrangements, such as estab­
lishing a joint land use planning 
committee to address development 
compatibility near their boundary, 
should be considered to avoid 
conflicts.
Village of Hobart
The Village of Hobart’s Comprehensive 
Plan shows future land use designations 
along the boundary with Green Bay as 
primarily being single-family residential 
and natural areas, with a small amount 
of agriculture. Several objectives and key 
points have been outlined below: 
	-
Explore mergers in public services to 
achieve cost-savings for both munici­
palities. 
	-
Maintain the Purchased Water Agree­
ment. 
	-
Seek mutual aid with Fire Departments.
	-
The Community Opinion Map indicates 
that “Light industrial and manufac­
turing uses are suited to areas Airport 
Drive and Florist Drive; along the 
border with Green Bay south of Mason 
Street; and, east of South Pine Tree 
Road between Fernando Drive and 
Orlando Drive.” However, this is not 
shown on the future land use map.
	-
The Village may consider participating 
in the Port of Green Bay’s plan imple­
mentation process to inform planners 
of its intentions to utilize the port and 
ensure modifications align with the 
Village’s long-term economic develop­
ment strategy. 
	-
Hobart’s proximity to Green Bay 
provides opportunities for directly 
marketing specialty agricultural prod­
ucts to local consumers.
