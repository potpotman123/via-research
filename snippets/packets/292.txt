ANALYSIS
In support of City Council’s "Enhancing Regional Mobility" priority, the City partnered with Via
Transportation, Inc., to launch an on-demand rideshare service on December 11, 2017 to 
offer an additional mobility option for residents, employees and visitors. The contract was 
renewed annually through 2022. At that time, a competitive procurement process was 
undertaken, River North Transit (Via Transportation) was selected and a two-year contract 
was executed. The two-year contract contains provisions for up to four, two-year renewals. 
This contract expires December 31, 2024.
The app-based Arlington On-Demand rideshare service currently uses a fleet of up to 65 six-
passenger vehicles, a smart phone application and dynamic routing to provide efficient on-
demand trips and access to a wide range of destinations. The current service area includes
the Arlington city limits, the CentrePort TRE Station, and two Trinity Metro bus lines located 
on Eastchase Parkway in Fort Worth. The current rate structure allows riders to access any 
destination within the service area from 6a.m. to 9p.m., Monday through Friday and from 
9a.m. to 9p.m. on Saturdays for $3.00 to $5.00 per person per trip, depending on distance 
traveled. Riders can also purchase a weekly ViaPass for a current cost of $25 for up to four 
rides per day for the week and a monthly ViaPass for a current cost of $80 for up to four rides
per day for 30 days. 
The total cost for two years of On-Demand rideshare service is $18,588,845.60. Funding for 
this service will come from a mix of farebox revenue, Federal Transit Administration (FTA)
funding, and City funding. In addition to the On-Demand rideshare service costs, four other 
related Via services are expected to be implemented through this contract renewal.

In October 2023, the City transitioned the Handitran elderly and disabled service to the Via 
software platform in order to provide a unified software platform for both the On-Demand 
rideshare service and the Handitran service. This unified software platform allows riders to 
access both services through one application, improves data reporting and analytics, and 
yields efficiencies. The total cost for two years of Handitran software services is $284,229.12.
The City was awarded a $1,126,873 grant from the North Central Texas Council of 
Governments (NCTCOG) to pilot a dynamic direct route service that will complement the 
existing On-Demand service. During periods of peak ridership demand, a number of vehicles 
will operate on a predictable schedule and route connecting downtown Arlington and the 
University of Texas at Arlington (UTA) campus with the CentrePort TRE Station. The dynamic 
direct route pilot will be designed to optimize service efficiency and provide a streamlined 
experience for riders to these common destinations. The pilot is expected to operate for three 
years. The total cost for two years of this service is $1,086,860.25. 
The City was also awarded a $4,560,000 grant from NCTCOG for the RAPID autonomous 
vehicle project. The City expects to use the NCTCOG grant funding to pay Via up to 
$816,713.35 during this contract term for the remaining scheduled deployment. No City funds 
are expected to be required for the NCTCOG funded project.
Finally, the City was awarded a $144,000 grant from the University of Minnesota for the 
Mobility, Access, and Transportation Insecurity Program. These funds will be used to support 
the Healthy Arlington Innovative Mobility (Healthy AIM) pilot project. Via will provide access 
to a custom booking tool that local healthcare nonprofit organizations can use to book rides 
on the Arlington On-Demand service for their clients. The City will pay Via up to $15,000 in 
grant funding for this project. 
The Texas Attorney General has previously ruled that certain information in the Via contract 
is confidential and cannot be disclosed to the public, and therefore a redacted copy of the 
contract is included in the agenda packet.
