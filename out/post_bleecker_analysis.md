# Via contract flow after the Bleecker report (2025-12-16 to 2026-09-20)

Question: has Via's new business become more SaaS-like since the short report? Answer from the documents: no. Of the 20 contract actions
dated after 2025-12-16 in the census, 12 are Turnkey, 1 is Hybrid (Rochester, MN: Via supplies operations on city-owned buses), 2 are
Software-only and 5 are Unknown. Every post-report action with a stated dollar value is an operations contract priced per vehicle hour, per
driver hour, or as a fixed operating fee. Documented software-only new business after the report carries no public dollar value at all.

Source table: `out/analysis_summary.md` §2 (regenerated from `out/via_contract_census.csv`). Dates are governing-body approval dates where
known, otherwise launch dates (noted).

## 6. Funding of post-report awards
Plano (FY2025-26 General Fund), Addison (Streets Self-Funded Projects Fund), Douglas County (Road and Bridge sales-and-use tax plus state
grants), Highland Park (FY26 budget amendment) are locally funded. Grand Prairie cites FY26 FTA 5307 funds; NJ Transit's pilot is a $7,000,000
FTA CRRSAA grant; SacRT's expansion is CPUC Access for All money; Grand County's shuttle is National Park Service (up to $1.5mm) and UDOT funds.
Across the whole census, 123 of 327 Via actions state a funding source: 32 federal or federally derived, 23 state, 41 local or general fund, 27 other.
Exposure to the 2026-09-30 IIJA authorization lapse is therefore real but partial; the DART-exit suburbs that produced the largest 2026 wins are
paying from local budgets.

## 8. Reading
The post-report contract flow is the opposite of a SaaS pivot. Via's 2026 wins are operations contracts, several bought off cooperative
schedules (Addison through the 791 cooperative via Tidewater Transit; Grand Prairie by cooperative purchase) that let cities contract without
their own RFP, which is how the DART-exit suburbs moved in weeks. The whole-system operations model (Sioux Falls 2024, Mobile 2025-09,
Rochester 2026-07, TCATA 2025-12) is the fastest-growing part of the documented base and is priced like a Transdev or MV contract. Meanwhile the
software competition is being lost: every documented competitor award since 2024 (Spare, RideCo) is a software contract at an agency where Via
either keeps the driving (LA Metro, Ben Franklin Transit) or exits entirely (Baldwin, Hillsborough, Met Council microtransit). The census
therefore supports Bleecker's characterization of what Via sells, and contradicts the inference that the business is shrinking: it is growing,
as a labor contractor.
